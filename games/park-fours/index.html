How it's drawn

  1. A tiny 3D park. Everything is built from boxes, rough spheres and one bumpy sheet of grass, lit by a sun and a sky light, with hard shadows.
  2. Drawn small on purpose. The camera looks down at a fixed angle with no perspective, and renders into a picture only a few hundred dots wide, with no smoothing.
  3. The pond mirror. Before the main picture, the trees, people and sky are drawn again upside down around the water line. The pond shows that picture with a slight ripple.
  4. Outlines from depth. Every dot knows how far away it is. If a neighbouring dot is more than half a metre closer, this dot gets darker, which draws a crisp edge around shapes.
  5. Snapping to the palette. Each colour is swapped for the closest of about 80 paint colours. Closeness is measured the way eyes judge colour, with lightness counting a bit more than hue.
  6. Dither. An optional 4×4 checker pattern nudges lightness up and down, so smooth light breaks into tidy patterns instead of bands.
  7. Blown up in whole pixels. Each dot becomes an exact square of screen pixels, and the camera snaps to that grid so the park doesn't shimmer.
